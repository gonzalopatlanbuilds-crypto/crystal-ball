# PERSONAS - Validator

Two formal personas. Persona 1 is built from my own direct experience (Concept G). Persona 2 is synthetic: built from public, verifiable market and regulatory patterns (cited below), not from an interview - no claim is made that she is a real person. Both will be updated as reality corrects them.

## Persona 1: Gonzalo, the founder doing it himself

- **Name / age:** Gonzalo, 22, founder
- **Context:** Runs Concept G, an organic coffee supply business (since Oct 2022). Works with 1-3 packaging suppliers, coordinated by email and WhatsApp. No dedicated compliance staff - he does this himself, alongside running the rest of the company.
- **The need, in his words:** "I need to know my packaging paperwork actually holds up before someone asks me to prove it, and I don't have a team to double-check it by hand every time a supplier sends a new file."
- **What he does today:** Keeps supplier emails and WhatsApp messages as the record. Compares numbers by eye when something looks off. No version history - if a supplier resends a corrected file, the old one is usually just gone.
- **What he'd pay:** MXN 1,000+/month if it credibly reduces legal/compliance risk - confirmed directly, conditional on the risk reduction being real, not just a dashboard.
- **What would make him quit:** If it generates noise (too many low-value findings) without ever catching something he would have missed, or if a finding turns out to be wrong and costs him trust in a real supplier relationship.

## Persona 2: Renata, compliance lead at a growing cosmetics brand

*Synthetic persona, not an interview. Built from: Mexico's General Law on Circular Economy (published Jan 19, 2026, in force Jan 20, 2026), under which Extended Producer Responsibility (REP) phases in sector by sector through SEMARNAT's general implementation agreements - still being drafted as of August 2026, which is why no company yet has a finished compliance process to copy; industry estimates that REP will represent 2-5% of product value and that initial technical redesign to comply can cost 5-12% (TheFoodTech, cosmetics/packaging trade press); and the existence of real Mexican companies already positioning themselves to help larger retailers (e.g., Bioelements, working with Walmart and Coppel per Cronista) - evidence that mid-size producers without that kind of partner are the ones left exposed.*

- **Name / age:** Renata, 34, compliance and operations lead
- **Context:** Works at a mid-size Mexican cosmetics brand (roughly 20-80 employees), selling through retail and e-commerce. Sources packaging from 5-8 suppliers across plastics, glass and cartons. No in-house environmental lawyer; compliance is one of several things she owns.
- **The need, in her words:** "The law is already in effect and retailers are starting to ask questions, but the actual rules for my category haven't been published yet, and I can't tell my supplier data is solid until I can trace every number back to where it came from."
- **What she does today:** A shared spreadsheet per supplier, manually reconciled before board or retailer reviews. No audit trail of who changed what. Considered an enterprise platform (Specright-class, ~US$60,000/year) and ruled it out as too expensive for her company's size.
- **What she'd pay:** More than Persona 1 - a mid-size brand with retail exposure (Walmart/Coppel-style buyers asking questions) has more at stake per discrepancy; exact willingness to pay is unconfirmed and should be tested, not assumed, before pricing.
- **What would make her quit:** If the tool can't keep up once her supplier count grows past what a small producer has, or if it gives her false confidence - a "clean" result that a retailer's own audit later contradicts.
